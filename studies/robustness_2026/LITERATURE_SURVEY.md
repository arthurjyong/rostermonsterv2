# Literature Survey: LAHC Tuning for Deployed Physician Rostering

_Status: v1.0, 2026-07-04. Built by a 5-agent deep-read workflow (wf_abd59e82-04b); full texts read where openly available, access level marked per finding. Companion to READING_LIST.md and PROTOCOL_DRAFT.md._

## A. Knowledge map (what the field actually knows)

The LAHC literature has settled qualitative laws but almost nothing at the statistical resolution we plan. Firmly established: convergence time grows approximately linearly in history length L with a measurable per-instance coefficient (Burke & Bykov CSM-192, replicated by Bazargani and, for SCHC, by Bykov & Petrovic at 1500+ runs); quality improves with L with diminishing returns on hard instances but is flat in L on roughly half of tested instances (6/12 ITC2007), with the flat/sensitive split predictable from the time/L coefficient, which also predicts whether multi-start beats one long run; under a fixed wall-clock budget the L-quality relation becomes non-monotone because large L fails to converge (Fonseca 2016); too-small L causes premature convergence that more iterations cannot repair (0/100 runs at L=1 matched L=50000 quality even at the full budget); and the acceptance/replacement variant matters as much as L, since DLAS and CLAHC shift the optimal L by 2-4 orders of magnitude (5-100 vs tens of thousands), while at least three non-equivalent canonical pseudocodes circulate (PATAT 2008 insert-always, EJOR 2017 replace-if-improving, Timefold step-based with acceptedCountLimit=1 and default size 400). The complete published record of L exploration is 3-9 coarse values or visual scatter plots of single runs; there are zero confidence intervals or equivalence tests on any L-response curve, no published tuning of the move mix (all works use fixed equal or hand-set probabilities, even though Franzin & Stützle's component analysis found the acceptance criterion and the LAHC history length among the highest-importance components), exactly one systematic idle-cutoff study (Bazargani's coupon-collector cutoff with a theta >= L floor, showing percentage rules do not transfer), and only indirect restart guidance. Head-to-head superiority claims are weak and problem-dependent: Burke & Bykov themselves said LAHC vs SA/TA results 'hardly represent strong evidence', and the one rigorously tuned comparison (Franzin & Stützle, irace + Friedman) found LAHC clearly best on PFSP-TCT, tied on structured QAP, mid-pack on random QAP, with tuned SA collapsing off-distribution while LAHC transferred.

The algorithm-configuration and statistics methodology literatures supply hard norms a reviewer will police: disjoint train/test instance sets never touched during tuning, selection on training performance only; blocking on identical instance+seed pairs with one run per pair spread over many instances (Birattari's variance result); tuning effort reported in solver-runs at equal budgets for every competitor (the codified answer to Hooker's 'we tuned ours harder' critique), with an identical pre-specified space-filling design explicitly blessed as fair by Bartz-Beielstein et al. 2020 when full per-algorithm tuning is infeasible; instance-level (not seed-level) units of analysis to avoid pseudoreplication (Campelo & Takahashi); Friedman omnibus plus Shaffer-static or Bergmann-Hommel adjusted p-values for all-pairwise comparisons (Nemenyi deprecated); power-based instance counts (about 34 instances for d*=0.5 at 80% power) and SE-controlled adaptive seed counts; and a mature best-of-K methodology (order-statistic expected-best curves from the empirical CDF, Dodge 2019, with Tang 2020's unbiased estimator, plus Hoos & Stützle's RTD tail-shape theory of when restarts help). Overtuning is measurable and usually mild but severe in about 10% of cases, aggravated by single instances and fixed seeds; perturbation-based instance generation from real parents is accepted practice (Styles & Hoos deleted 10-30% of TSPLIB nodes), with the discipline of keeping children of one parent on one side of the split. Equivalence testing (TOST/ROPE) has essentially no footprint in optimization benchmarking; the only quantitative precedent is Benavoli's classification-specific 1% ROPE, so an equivalence framing would be a methodological novelty requiring an operationally justified margin.

The rostering field has a mature benchmark culture (INRC-I/II, schedulingbenchmarks.org) with a settled hierarchy on benchmarks: mathematical programming won both nurse rostering competitions, with tuned SA trailing by roughly 4-15% in cost; outside competitions, comparative practice is one hand-picked configuration and a single seed (Burke & Curtois say so verbatim), and the major reviews (Van den Bergh 2013: 46 of ~300 papers 'applied in practice'; Erhard 2018; Abdullah 2025) document single-institution non-public data, self-implemented baselines, and an explicit unmet call for standardized comparative validation. Statistically principled tuning exists only at the margins (Ceschia & Schaerf's F-Race-tuned SA on INRC-II competition instances); no located study combines rigorous tuning with fair multi-algorithm comparison on production instances from a deployed system, and no rostering study tunes LAHC specifically. Deployed-system papers form a separate genre whose evidence currency is time-to-roster and satisfaction surveys (ROTA: 45 to 10 min/day, 91% satisfaction; HealthRota: SUS 86/100), with zero optimization rigor; the most recent deployed physician MIP paper (arXiv 2511.14536) uses 12 monthly real instances and no statistical tests, calibrating our planned instance count as normal. Weighted-sum multi-component objectives like our 9-component score are the dominant formulation, but the fairness literature warns they yield solver-dependent fairness and recommends independent post-hoc fairness statistics.

## B. Open gaps we can credibly fill

### [HIGH] No statistically characterized L-response curve exists for LAHC: prior guidance is 3-9 coarse values or visual single-run scatter plots, with zero confidence intervals and zero equivalence testing, and the founders' own idle-cutoff/L interaction question (PATAT 2008) has exactly one follow-up (Bazargani's CCP cutoff).

**Why it is open:** Synthesis across every obtainable LAHC primary source (Burke & Bykov 2008/2012-2017, Bykov & Petrovic 2016, Fonseca 2016, Namazi 2018, Bazargani 2017/2021, Clay 2021, Franzin & Stützle 2017/2019): the densest published sweep is ~500 random-L single-run points analyzed purely visually; Fonseca's 9-value sweep used 5 runs per cell under a confounding fixed timeout and no tests. Franzin & Stützle independently found the LAHC history length to be the single most important numerical parameter in their framework (16% importance on PFSP-TCT), confirming the parameter is worth a dedicated study.

**Our fit:** Strong. We control the full solver, can run tens of thousands of budget-matched runs on real plus perturbed production instances, log the DLAS stagnation diagnostic and the time/L angle coefficient nobody has reported alongside a sweep, and hold CP-SAT bounds to anchor quality. The single-ward homogeneity that limits generality is exactly what makes a high-resolution response surface estimable. The flat-regime risk (half of ITC2007 instances were L-insensitive) is absorbed by designing for equivalence bands: a CI-backed 'quality is flat over L in [a,b], so use small L and converge fast' is itself the practice-relevant finding, and locating the industrial default (Timefold L=400) on the measured curve is a practice-facing result no one has produced.

### [HIGH] No fairly-tuned multi-algorithm comparison exists on production rostering instances from a deployed system: competition protocols compare untuned or self-tuned entries on synthetic instances; deployed-system papers do no algorithmic comparison at all; the two genres have never been combined.

**Why it is open:** Van den Bergh 2013 (~300 papers): comparisons are typically against 'some basic tabu search or simulated annealing', one config, one seed; Abdullah 2025 (60 physician papers) states lack of comparative validation as a top future-work priority; the closest analogues are Ceschia & Schaerf (F-Race-tuned SA, but on INRC-II competition instances) and Knust & Xie 2019 (deployed SA + MIP bounds, tuning rigor unverified because paywalled). Franzin & Stützle's rigorous acceptance-criterion comparison used QAP/PFSP benchmarks, not scheduling deployments.

**Our fit:** Good, with caveats. We have the deployed system, production instances, one fixed scoring function (mirroring the INRC single-validator design), CP-SAT as yardstick, and the compute to give every algorithm an identical pre-specified DOE budget (the Bartz-Beielstein-sanctioned fair design). The caveat: the methodological template already exists (Franzin & Stützle), so our claim must be scoped as first-in-domain (physician rostering, production instances, deployment) plus the equivalence framing, not first-ever fair comparison. Novelty is contingent on checking Knust & Xie and Kletzander & Musliu full texts first.

### [MEDIUM] No published LAHC work tunes or even sweeps the move-operator mix: every paper in the canon uses a small fixed random mixture with equal or hand-set probabilities ('adjusted based on empirical observation', Fonseca), despite component-analysis evidence that neighborhood exploration is among the highest-leverage components.

**Why it is open:** Direct reading of the move-set sections of CSM-192, Bykov & Petrovic 2016, and Fonseca 2016: three to six operators, probabilities fixed by hand, never varied experimentally; no LAHC paper reports a move-mix experiment of any kind.

**Our fit:** Moderate. Our mix is only two operators (swap/reassign), so the design is a clean one-dimensional sweep crossable with L, cheap to run and easy to analyze for interaction effects. The thinness is also a limitation: a 2-operator mix on one problem class is a modest contribution on its own, so it should be framed as one axis of the response surface, not a headline claim.

### [MEDIUM] Restart policy for LAHC (best-of-K vs one long run) has only indirect and partly conflicting guidance, and no one has applied the modern order-statistic best-of-K methodology to metaheuristic restarts with instance blocking and equivalence margins.

**Why it is open:** SCHC's T/Lc heuristic predicts the multi-start/long-run split but was never tested statistically; Bazargani's mLAHC vs pLAHC comparison found similar final quality with landscape-dependent seeding effects; Hoos & Stützle's RTD tail-shape theory and Dodge/Tang's expected-best curves exist but were developed for SLS runtime and NLP hyperparameter trials respectively, never combined on scheduling metaheuristics.

**Our fit:** Good as an application, weak as a novelty claim: every analytical piece is published, so the contribution is the assembly (expected-best-of-K curves from pooled production-instance runs, unbiased estimator, tail-shape diagnosis, budget-dependent crossover reporting) plus testing the T/Lc prediction on a new problem class. Position it as a decision recipe for practitioners (given your measured time/L coefficient, restart or not), not as new methodology.

### [MEDIUM] Equivalence testing (TOST/ROPE) with an operationally justified margin has never been used in metaheuristic tuning or comparison: the field's entire inferential toolbox is difference testing, and the only ROPE precedent (Benavoli's 1%) is classification-specific and flagged as non-transferable by its own authors.

**Why it is open:** Negative finding from systematic search across algorithm-configuration and benchmarking literature (July 2026); TOST absent from Bartz-Beielstein 2020, López-Ibáñez 2016, Hutter 2009, Eggensperger 2019; Carrasco 2020 explicitly warns the 1% ROPE default may not fit other domains.

**Our fit:** Distinctive and feasible, but only because our deployment grounds the margin: we can define the minimally relevant effect size in roster-score units the ICU operators actually care about (e.g., the weight of one additional undesirable assignment), which a benchmark-only study cannot do credibly. This is the piece that turns expected flat/null results in the L sweep and the algorithm comparison into positive, tested claims. Risk: reviewers may attack any margin as arbitrary; mitigate by pre-registering it and deriving it from the score weights and operator judgments.

## C. Research questions for the paper

- RQ1 (primary): On production physician-rostering instances, what is the joint response surface of LAHC solution quality and time-to-convergence over history length L and idle cutoff, with confidence intervals and pre-registered equivalence bands; over what L range is quality practically equivalent; does the canonical linear time-in-L law with a stable per-instance angle coefficient hold; and where does the industrial default (Timefold Late Acceptance, size 400, acceptedCountLimit 1) sit on that curve?
- RQ2: Under equal move-count budgets and identically designed tuning, are LAHC, simulated annealing, step-counting hill climbing, tabu search, and random-restart hill climbing practically equivalent (TOST within an operationally defined roster-score margin) or statistically distinguishable in gap-to-CP-SAT-bound on this problem class, and does the ranking change across practitioner time budgets (30 s, 10 min, production budget)?
- RQ3: Does best-of-K restarting beat one long run on these instances, analyzed as expected-best-of-K curves with budget-dependent crossovers; and do the published predictors (per-instance time/L coefficient, run-quality distribution tail shape) correctly predict which regime wins?
- RQ4: Do tuned configurations transfer: how much overtuning (Schneider incumbent-trajectory statistic, train/test Spearman) occurs when tuning on perturbed instances and deploying on held-out real months, and is the optimal (L, cutoff, move mix) stable across the real-vs-perturbed and month-to-month instance family?
- RQ5 (secondary): Does the swap/reassign move mix materially affect quality at fixed budget, and does it interact with L, providing the first designed move-mix experiment in the LAHC literature?

## D. Mandated revisions to our study design

1. Pin the exact LAHC variant in pseudocode (acceptance test, replacement rule, initialization, steps vs raw evaluations, acceptedCountLimit): three non-equivalent canonical versions circulate and DLAS/CLAHC show the replacement rule alone shifts optimal L by 2-4 orders of magnitude, so unpinned results are non-transferable and non-comparable to the L=400 industrial default.
2. Never sweep L under a single fixed wall-clock budget: Fonseca shows that confounds convergence and makes quality-vs-L non-monotone. Either run to convergence under a proportional idle cutoff and report time as a second response, or make (L, budget) an explicit factorial.
3. Redesign the idle-cutoff axis: enforce cutoff >= L (Bazargani's floor), test proportional (2%) and CCP-style neighborhood-scaled cutoffs rather than fixed idle counts, denominate budgets in moves/evaluations not CPU seconds (machine independence, Eggensperger 5.8), and frame the cutoff as an anytime-behavior parameter inside a fixed outer budget so it cannot act as a budget cheat.
4. Design for equivalence, not just difference: pre-specify a minimally relevant effect size in roster-score units justified from operational meaning (Lakens strategies), use it for both power analysis and TOST/ROPE margins, and report conclusions in the 2x2 different/equivalent frame. Expect the flat regime (half of ITC2007 instances were L-insensitive) and treat a defended null as a headline result.
5. Restructure the experimental unit: block on instance+seed pairs shared across all configurations and algorithms, prefer one run per pair across many perturbed instances over many seeds on the real instance (Birattari variance result), analyze per-instance paired summaries only (pseudoreplication), treat base month as a blocking/random factor because perturbations of one parent are correlated, and hold out disjoint seeds for final evaluation.
6. Add a train/test discipline the current draft lacks: split instances before any experiment, keep all children of one parent roster on one side, select configurations on training performance only, and report train/test Spearman plus the Schneider overtuning statistic; justify instance count via Kendall's W homogeneity (irace method) and the Campelo-Takahashi power machinery (~30-40 instances for d*=0.5 at 80% power, or publish the power curve).
7. For best-of-K restarts, replace any 'best of K runs' point estimate with expected-best-of-K curves from order statistics on the pooled runs, using or citing the unbiased expected-maximum estimator (Tang et al.), with selection on training performance only, plus per-instance quality-distribution tail-shape checks (Hoos & Stützle) and the SCHC T/Lc coefficient as a pre-registered predictor.
8. Expand the baseline set: Timefold default (LA, size 400, acceptedCountLimit 1), DLAS size 5 (already a Timefold preview feature), pLAHC doubling restarts as an auto-tuned baseline (published overhead 1.5-4.5x), uniformly random configurations at equal budget (irace warns tuning can fail to beat random), and the current production configuration.
9. For the cross-algorithm arm, adopt the Franzin & Stützle protocol (shared code skeleton, identical neighborhoods and termination, equal per-algorithm tuning budgets in solver-runs, ARPD vs CP-SAT bound, Friedman + Shaffer-static or Bergmann-Hommel adjusted p-values, never Nemenyi), set SA initial temperature by the 85% acceptance convention or tune it, and note in the paper that the fixed 9-component score's scale is a documented SA sensitivity that LAHC provably lacks (rescaling-invariance results).
10. Report at multiple time budgets (30 s and 10 min practitioner conventions plus the production budget): INRC-I ran three budget tracks because rankings are budget-dependent, and our restart and cutoff answers will be too.
11. Instrument every run with the two published mechanistic diagnostics no tuning paper has reported alongside a sweep: the DLAS stagnation fraction (% of iterations in the HC-like state) and the per-instance time/L angle coefficient; these turn observed failures of specific (L, cutoff) cells into explanations rather than anomalies.
12. Add rostering-genre reporting the OR-health venues expect: field-standard instance descriptors, per-component violation counts (weighted totals mislead when weights differ by orders of magnitude, Burke & Curtois WHPP anomaly), independent post-hoc fairness statistics (std dev / range / Jain index of per-doctor burden) since weighted-sum-only fairness is criticized as solver-dependent, deployment evidence in the genre currency (months live, time-to-roster, operator satisfaction), and released anonymized instances answering the most-cited gap in both physician-scheduling reviews.
13. Verify the final configuration ranking on the production Cloud Run environment if tuning runs elsewhere (Eggensperger Pitfall 7: rankings inverted across machine types); move-count budgets immunize most metrics but any wall-clock-coupled cutoff re-imports machine dependence.

## E. Threats to the contribution claim

- Knust & Xie (Annals of OR 2019) is the closest analogue (SA + MIP bounds on real instances inside deployed commercial rostering software) and its full text is unread (paywalled): if their tuning was statistically principled, our 'first rigorous tuning on production rostering instances' claim collapses to a narrower one. Obtain via institutional access before writing the novelty paragraph; same for Kletzander & Musliu 2020 and Cildoz et al.
- Franzin & Stützle (2017/2019) already delivered a rigorously tuned acceptance-criterion comparison including LAHC with irace and Friedman tests, and found the answer is problem-dependent: our comparison arm is not methodologically novel and must be positioned as first-in-domain with an equivalence framing, or a reviewer will read it as a replication on a new instance set.
- The flat-regime risk is real and quantified: on 6 of 12 ITC2007 instances final quality was insensitive to L over huge ranges, and small-L insensitivity is also documented. If our instances are flat, the L-sweep 'tuning' contribution survives only if the design was pre-registered for equivalence claims; a difference-testing design would return an unpublishable null.
- MP dominance on the field's flagship benchmarks (branch-and-price and IP won both INRCs; tuned SA trailed by 4-15%): if CP-SAT solves our production instances to optimality within the production budget, the referee question becomes 'why tune a metaheuristic at all'. We must either show CP-SAT cannot meet the operational budget/incrementality requirements or reframe LAHC as the anytime engine with CP-SAT as certifier.
- Variant specificity caps generality: our results will hold for our exact LAHC variant (likely Timefold-style step-based) and DLAS/CLAHC prove optimal L does not transfer across replacement rules; a reviewer can legitimately bound our claims to one variant on one problem class at one site.
- Ceschia & Schaerf's F-Race-tuned SA (99% confidence) already exists in nurse rostering: 'first statistically principled tuning in rostering' is false; only 'on production physician instances from a deployed system' survives, and only as 'to our knowledge'.
- Correlated pseudo-instances: all our perturbed instances descend from a handful of production months from one ward; naive treatment inflates Type I error (Nadeau-Bengio/Dietterich lesson) and a sharp statistical reviewer will check. Month-as-block and parent-side splits are mandatory, and even then generality beyond this ward/scoring function is limited by design.
- The best-of-K analysis machinery is entirely published (Hoos & Stützle 1998, Dodge 2019, Tang 2020, Bazargani 2021); claiming it as a contribution rather than an application invites an easy rejection point.
- Equivalence-margin attack: no precedent exists for a ROPE in scheduling objectives, so the margin will be scrutinized; if it is not pre-registered and operationally derived (and ideally corroborated by operator judgment), the TOST framing becomes the paper's weakest point rather than its novelty.
- Genre mismatch at the target venues: Health Care Management Science and ORHC referees may want the deployment/impact story (the ROTA/HealthRota evidence currency) while our core is algorithm statistics; a paper that is 80% tuning methodology may be redirected to a metaheuristics venue (EJOR/COR/J. Heuristics), so the framing must integrate both evidence types deliberately.
- Field velocity: the 2025 IEEE Access review and the Nov 2025 deployed physician-MIP framework show active movement toward deployed physician-rostering papers; a competing group adding statistical rigor to a deployment could pre-empt the domain claim during our writing cycle.

# Theme digests (evidence base)

## LAHC: the algorithm, its parameters, variants, and failure modes

**State of knowledge:** The LAHC literature firmly establishes qualitative laws but almost nothing at the statistical resolution we plan. Firmly known: (1) convergence time grows approximately linearly in L with a per-instance coefficient (Burke & Bykov's 500-run sweeps; replicated at 100 runs by Bazargani; replicated for SCHC at 1500+ runs); (2) final quality improves with L with diminishing returns on 'hard' instances but is essentially FLAT in L on a substantial fraction of instances (6 of 12 ITC2007 instances in the SCHC study), and the flat/sensitive split correlates with the measured time/L coefficient, which doubles as a hardness proxy and a multi-start-vs-long-run decision rule; (3) under a fixed wall-clock budget the L-quality relation becomes non-monotone because large L fails to converge (Fonseca); (4) too-small L causes genuine premature convergence that cannot be bought back with more iterations (0/100 runs at L=1 matched the L=50000 quality even with the full L=50000 budget); (5) the characteristic stagnation mode is the history array filling with the newest best cost, after which LAHC provably behaves like HC, measured at 21-65% of iterations on TSP even at L=50000 (DLAS); and (6) the acceptance/replacement variant matters as much as L itself: replacement-rule tweaks (DLAS, CLAHC) shift the optimal L from tens of thousands to 5-100, and at least three non-equivalent 'canonical' LAHC pseudocodes circulate (PATAT 2008 insert-always; EJOR 2017 replace-only-if-improving; Timefold step-based with acceptedCountLimit=1 and default size 400).

Contested or absent: head-to-head superiority claims are weak. Burke & Bykov themselves concluded LAHC-vs-SA/TA results 'hardly represent strong evidence of superiority of either' (no tests); SCHC beats LAHC on longer runs on ITC2007 (no tests); the only rigorously tuned comparison (Franzin & Stützle, irace + Friedman) finds LAHC clearly best on PFSP-TCT, tied for best on structured QAP, and mid-pack on random QAP, i.e. problem-dependent. Statistical practice in the primary LAHC literature is thin: the founding papers contain zero hypothesis tests; later works use occasional t-tests/Wilcoxon on algorithm contrasts. No published work sweeps L with confidence intervals or equivalence testing; no work tunes the move mix (all use fixed equal or hand-set probabilities); the idle-cutoff x L interaction has exactly one systematic treatment (Bazargani's coupon-collector cutoff with the theta >= L floor, showing fixed-percentage idle rules do not transfer across instances or parameter settings); and restart policy (best-of-K) has only indirect guidance (SCHC's T/Lc heuristic, mLAHC-vs-pLAHC anytime curves, and the landscape-dependent seeding result). The planned study therefore fills real, citable gaps on all four of its axes (L, idle cutoff, move mix, restarts), provided it pins its exact LAHC variant and treats wall-clock budget as a design variable rather than a nuisance.

**Design implications:**
- Specify the exact LAHC variant in pseudocode (acceptance test, replacement rule, initialization, and whether L counts raw candidate evaluations or Timefold-style steps with acceptedCountLimit=1): at least three non-equivalent canonical versions circulate, and DLAS/CLAHC show the replacement rule alone shifts optimal L by 2-4 orders of magnitude, so results are not transferable across variants.
- Expect and test for the flat regime: on half the ITC2007 instances final quality was insensitive to L over huge ranges. Design the L sweep for equivalence testing (TOST or CI-on-difference bands), not just difference testing; a null result over a wide L band is itself the publishable, practice-relevant finding (it licenses small L and fast convergence).
- Never sweep L under a single fixed wall-clock budget alone: Fonseca shows that makes quality-vs-L non-monotone via the convergence confound. Either run to convergence with a proportional idle cutoff and report time as a second response, or make (L, budget) a factorial design.
- Tie the idle cutoff to L and neighborhood size: enforce cutoff >= L (Bazargani's theta >= Lh floor), prefer proportional (2% Burke-Bykov, 1% SCHC) or CCP-based cutoffs theta = max(L, beta*|N|*ln|N|), and expect the best cutoff to differ between real and perturbed instances; a fixed idle count or fixed percentage will not transfer.
- Measure the per-instance time/L 'angle coefficient' from short runs: it is (a) the published recipe for setting L to fill a time budget, (b) a hardness proxy, and (c) the published predictor of whether best-of-K restarts beats one long run, which is exactly our restart-arm hypothesis; report it for the production instance and perturbations.
- Log the DLAS stagnation diagnostic (% of iterations in the HC-like state where the history array equals the current best) during all runs: it is a cheap, published mechanistic explanation for why a given L or cutoff fails, and no tuning paper has yet reported it alongside an L sweep.
- For the best-of-K restart arm, include (a) fixed-L multistart (mLAHC-style), (b) doubling-L restarts (pLAHC) as an auto-tuned baseline whose published overhead is 1.5-4.5x tuned LAHC, and treat warm-starting/seeding as landscape-dependent (helped TSP, failed QAP/PFSP), not an obvious win.
- For the cross-algorithm extension, copy the Franzin & Stützle protocol: one shared code skeleton, identical neighborhood and termination, per-algorithm tuning with equal irace/tuner budgets, ARPD vs the CP-SAT yardstick, Friedman rank-sum with significance thresholds plus per-pair Wilcoxon; set SA initial temperature by initial acceptance ratio (the 85% convention) or tune it, and note that our fixed 9-component weighted score gives SA a scale-sensitivity handicap that LAHC/SCHC provably do not have (rescaling-invariance experiments in both Burke-Bykov and Bykov-Petrovic).
- Benchmark against the industrial default explicitly: Timefold's Late Acceptance with size 400 steps and acceptedCountLimit 1 is the de facto practitioner configuration; showing where the default sits on our measured L-response curve is a high-value, practice-facing result. Include DLAS (size 5) as a cheap extra competitor since it is already a Timefold preview feature.
- Sample sizes and protocol norms from the literature: 20 runs (founding papers) is now below par; use 30-100 runs per configuration (CLAHC used 30 with Wilcoxon/Friedman, Bazargani 100), set per-instance equal-time budgets via a published protocol (e.g. DLAS's trapped-for-10%-of-runtime rule), and report means with dispersion, not best-of.
- Claim the gaps accurately in the paper: prior L guidance is 3-9 coarse values or visual scatter plots with zero confidence intervals; no prior move-mix tuning exists in the LAHC literature (fixed equal or hand-set probabilities only, while Franzin & Stützle found neighborhood exploration among the most important components); exactly one prior idle-cutoff study exists. These are citable novelty statements.
- When perturbing production instances, remember tuned parameters generalize poorly off-distribution for SA-family methods (Franzin: tuned SA collapsed to 9-10% ARPD on out-of-training sizes while LAHC stayed at 0.6%): evaluate tuned configurations on held-out perturbations, not just the instances they were tuned on.

**Findings:**

- **[READ-FULL]** Original LAHC formulation: accept candidate if C(s*) <= C_hat_v where v = I mod L; the new current cost is inserted into the list every iteration. Tested on 13 Toronto exam benchmarks, 20 runs each, with exactly three L settings: L=1 (pure HC), L=500, and a hand-customized L per instance (range 100 to 120,000) chosen to make runs last about 10 minutes. Average cost improved monotonically with L on 12 of 13 instances (e.g. Car92: 4.52 at L=1/34s, 4.08 at L=500/184s, 3.92 at L=2000/604s). The exception was Pur93 (very large instance): the 10-minute-budget L=100 run (avg 4.71) was worse than L=500 run to convergence (4.39, 3332s). No statistical tests of any kind; only best/average of 20 runs.
  - _Source:_ Burke & Bykov, 'A Late Acceptance Strategy in Hill-Climbing for Exam Timetabling Problems', PATAT 2008, https://patatconference.org/patat2008/proceedings/Bykov-HC2a.pdf
  - _Relevance:_ The founding paper's L guidance is 3 coarse settings and hand-tuning to a wall-clock budget, with zero inferential statistics. Any fine-grained, CI-backed L sweep we run is novel relative to the origin paper.
- **[READ-FULL]** The PATAT 2008 paper terminates on 50,000 consecutive idle (non-improving) moves and explicitly flags the idle-cutoff/L interaction as an open problem: 'when the list is quite long the presented algorithm tends to make an extremely slow improvement in the final phase... sometimes 50000 idle moves were not sufficient for the recognition of true convergence. This number should probably be calculated as a percentage of the total number of moves, but this issue needs to be more deeply studied.'
  - _Source:_ Burke & Bykov, PATAT 2008, final discussion section
  - _Relevance:_ Direct precedent for our idle-cutoff-vs-L question: the founders themselves observed a fixed idle count fails at large L and proposed proportional cutoffs, but never studied it statistically.
- **[READ-FULL]** Canonical LAHC (tech report CSM-192, June 2012; textually the basis of EJOR 258(1):70-78, 2017): final acceptance rule is C*_i <= C_{i-L} OR C*_i <= C_{i-1} (Formula 2), with the fitness array written each iteration in the TR pseudocode. L=0 or 1 reduces to greedy HC. Properties established: (1) longer list gives slower cost drop, observed in all experiments; (2) clear convergence; (3) in ~500 runs with L drawn randomly from [1, 50000] on Exam_1, final cost decreases in L with diminishing returns (scatter plot only, single-run points, no CIs), and on that instance 'any result with Lfa=50000 is better (in spite of the scatter) than any result with Lfa=5000'; (4) convergence time is approximately LINEAR in L, with a per-instance 'angle coefficient' Time/L that is constant per instance (exam instances: Nit/Lfa from 103 to 11,652) and for TSP scales as size^1.2 (Nit/Lfa/size^1.2 = 3.07 to 3.78 across 7 TSPLIB instances of 783-3795 cities).
  - _Source:_ Burke & Bykov, 'The Late Acceptance Hill-Climbing Heuristic', Univ. of Stirling Tech Report CSM-192, 2012, https://kjt.stir.ac.uk/research/techreps/pdf/TR192.pdf (= EJOR 2017 content)
  - _Relevance:_ This is the strongest published L-vs-quality and L-vs-time evidence: a 500-run random sweep, but analyzed purely visually. The linear time-in-L law plus per-instance angle coefficient is a concrete quantitative hypothesis our study can test with proper statistics, and gives a practical recipe (measure coefficient in a short run, set L to fill the time budget).
- **[READ-FULL]** CSM-192/EJOR termination convention: stop when consecutive non-improving (idle) iterations exceed 2% of total iterations elapsed (e.g. after 10^6 iterations, stop if last 20,000 idle). The authors note maximum effectiveness is achieved by stopping exactly at the intersection of the time-cost curve with the envelope of all curves, but say 'we currently have no reliable method to detect these points during the search', so convergence-point stopping is 'an acceptable choice'.
  - _Source:_ Burke & Bykov, CSM-192 (2012), Section 3.3
  - _Relevance:_ The canonical idle cutoff is proportional (2%), not absolute, and was adopted specifically because of the L interaction; our idle-cutoff tuning should treat the cutoff as proportional and expected to interact with L.
- **[READ-FULL]** CSM-192 comparison methodology and results vs SA/TA/GDA: same software, same move set, ~500 runs per algorithm with time-affecting parameters randomized so total iterations spread over 1-240 million; results binned into 12 segments and compared by segment-average cost ('cut-offs').   - _Relevance:_ Template and cautionary tale for our fairly-tuned algorithm comparison: (a) equalize compute and randomize/tune time-affecting parameters; (b) the headline claim 'LAHC roughly ties SA/TA, beats GDA' was never established with statistics; (c) SA results are sensitive to cost-function scale while LAHC is not, which matters because our 9-component weighted score fixes a scale that SA's temperature must be tuned to.
- **[READ-FULL]** Move operators in the LAHC/SCHC canon are always a small fixed random mixture, never systematically tuned: CSM-192 TSP uses 3 move types selected randomly (double-bridge, city swap, city reinsertion); CSM-192 exam timetabling uses 3 (room reallocation, Kempe-chain exam move, timeslot swap); SCHC uses 4 'selected randomly in equal proportions'; Fonseca et al. use 6 with hand-set probabilities (ES=0.20, EM=0.38, EBS=0.10, RS=0.20, RM=0.10, KM=0.02) that were 'adjusted based on empirical observation'. - **[READ-FULL]** Documented LAHC failure mode (stagnation): when a new best (local optimum) is found, its cost value fills the whole history array, after which LAHC behaves exactly like HC. Measured on TSPLIB instances with L=50000: LAHC behaved like HC in 21-65% of iterations (per-instance, Table 1), SCHC in 36-72%; on QAPLIB about 10% (LAHC) and 20% (SCHC). - **[READ-FULL]** DLAS experimental protocol for setting per-instance time budgets: run LAHC (L=50000) 50 times, stopping each run when it has been trapped in a local optimum for at least 10% of total running time; take the longest of the 50 running times as the instance's time cutoff for all compared algorithms; then 50 runs per algorithm per L, reporting means, with t-tests at 0.95 on the winner.

- **[READ-FULL]** SCHC (step counting hill climbing): keeps a cost bound Bc fixed for Lc consecutive steps (accept if C(s*) < Bc or C(s*) <= C(s)); variants count all moves (SCHC-all), accepted moves (SCHC-acp) or improving moves (SCHC-imp). Search time is approximately proportional to Lc, as for LAHC. Crucially, over >1500 runs per instance with randomized Lc, the Lc-vs-final-cost diagrams split into two shapes: 6 of 12 ITC2007 instances show clear quality improvement with larger Lc, and 6 show a FLAT response ('the average and the best costs are almost the same either with smaller Lc or with larger Lc'), in which case 'a more effective strategy is to produce multiple short runs and pick up the best result'. ## Algorithm configuration, tuning fairness, and generalization

**State of knowledge:** The algorithm-configuration field has hard, convergent norms that a journal reviewer from this community will expect: (1) instances are always split into disjoint training and test sets, the test set is never touched during tuning, and generalization is reported as test-set performance of a configuration selected purely on training performance; (2) evaluation is blocked on identical instance+seed pairs across configurations, one run per instance-seed pair (the Birattari 2004 variance result), with seeds never fixed globally and never tunable; (3) tuning effort is quantified in target-algorithm runs (irace case studies: 5,000-10,000 runs), and claims about tuning protocols rest on 16-30 independent tuner repetitions with bootstrap or Wilcoxon-type analysis; (4) fair algorithm comparison means tuning every competitor with the same procedure and budget - the codified answer to Hooker's 1995 'we tuned ours harder' critique - and the community's flagship benchmarking survey explicitly blesses an identical space-filling design (LHD/DOE) over each algorithm's parameter space as 'still fair' when full per-algorithm tuning is infeasible; (5) over-tuning is real, measurable, and usually mild but severe in roughly 10% of cases (Schneider et al.'s reanalysis; Eggensperger's train/test Spearman correlations from 0.99 to 0.15 show the spread is scenario-dependent), with small/heterogeneous instance sets and single seeds as the known aggravators, and repeated resampling and larger instance sets as the measured mitigations. Perturbation-based generation of instance families from a small set of real instances is an accepted, peer-reviewed practice (Styles & Hoos built 'TSPLIB-like' instances by deleting 10-30% of nodes precisely because 111 real instances were too few for configuration), with the accompanying discipline of reporting difficulty comparability and keeping generated sets disjoint across splits.

What is contested or absent: there is no absolute standard for how large a fair tuning budget is - only rules of thumb (Eggensperger: budget >= default-config runtime on 200-1000 instances; homogeneous sets may need only ~50 instances) - so budget parity plus explicit reporting, not budget size, is what gets policed. Inference inside tuners is deliberately heuristic (irace applies uncorrected tests at alpha=0.05 as a selection device), so statistical rigor lives at the final evaluation, and even there the field is entirely difference-testing based: we found no use of formal equivalence testing (TOST) anywhere in algorithm configuration or metaheuristics benchmarking, making a TOST-framed 'fairly-tuned algorithms are practically equivalent' claim novel. Configuration transfer across instance difficulty/distribution is known to be fragile (Styles & Hoos: the standard protocol selected configurations worse than default under shift), which is the main threat to tuning on perturbed instances and deploying on the real one. Finally, nothing in this literature addresses single-production-instance scheduling deployments directly - the closest analogues are the single-instance seed-overtuning experiment (Eggensperger Pitfall 5) and HPO's single-dataset setting (Schneider), both of which show the failure mode our design must explicitly measure.

**Design implications:**
- Report the tuning budget in solver-runs (configuration x instance x seed evaluations) and give every algorithm in the comparison exactly the same budget and the same pre-specified sweep/design; cite Hooker-1995-via-Eggensperger and Bartz-Beielstein et al. 2020 Section 7.5, which explicitly endorses an identical space-filling DOE as fair when full per-algorithm tuning is infeasible. A scripted, pre-registered DOE sweep is defensible against 'why not irace?' on three grounds: our goal is response-surface characterization (racing deliberately starves poor regions of evaluations, destroying surface information), the parameter space is tiny (~4 parameters, where irace would run only ~4 iterations), and the DOE is applied identically to all competitors; still, consider running irace once per algorithm at equal budget as a robustness check that our swept optima are not artifacts of the grid.
- Split instances into disjoint train/test sets before any experiment and never evaluate the test set during tuning; when perturbed instances are generated from the same real parent roster, keep all children of one parent (and the parent itself) on the same side of the split to prevent structural leakage (pattern follows Styles & Hoos's disjoint TSPLIB-like sets).
- Adopt perturbation-based instance generation openly with the two disciplines from the LION 2012 precedent: report the empirical difficulty/structure spread of perturbed vs parent instances (e.g., default-configuration score distributions), and validate the finally selected configurations on held-out real or real-parented instances before making any deployment claim, because configuration transfer across distributions is the documented failure mode.
- Block on instance+seed: give every configuration and every algorithm the same list of <instance, seed> pairs (ParamILS Section 5.1.2, irace Appendix A); prefer one run per <instance, seed> pair across many perturbed instances over many repeats on the single real instance (Birattari 2004 variance result), and hold out a disjoint set of seeds for final evaluation so seed-generalization (Eggensperger Pitfall 5) is measured separately from instance-generalization.
- Measure and report overtuning rather than assuming its absence: plot train vs test scores of all evaluated configurations with Spearman correlation (Eggensperger Figure 6 template) and report the incumbent-trajectory overtuning statistic of Schneider et al. (test performance of the training-selected incumbent vs best earlier incumbent); expect mild effects but design to detect the ~10% severe case.
- Justify the instance count via measured homogeneity: compute Kendall's W concordance of instance rankings under ~100 random configurations (irace's method); if W is high (plausible for one ward's rosters), ~50-200 instances is citable as sufficient (Eggensperger Section 5.3); if W is low, the whole single-configuration tuning premise weakens and per-instance-class analysis is needed.
- For the best-of-K restarts component, copy the ParamILS/Styles bootstrap protocol: run a large pool of independent restarts, then bootstrap-sample K of them (100,000 resamples), selecting the best on TRAINING performance only, and report the test-performance distribution of the selection as a function of K; selecting on test performance is explicitly named as peeking and will be caught by reviewers.
- Handle the idle-cutoff parameter carefully: it is a stopping criterion, so fix the outer budget (wall-clock or, better, total move/iteration count - Eggensperger 5.8 prefers elementary-operation budgets as machine-independent) and treat idle cutoff as an anytime-behavior parameter within that fixed budget; never let configurations with different effective budgets be compared as if equal, and never include seeds or semantics-changing parameters (e.g., feasibility thresholds among the 9 score components) in the tuned space.
- Use difference tests (Friedman + post-hoc across instances, or paired Wilcoxon) for the primary comparison since that is the community standard, and position any TOST equivalence analysis as an explicit methodological contribution with a pre-registered equivalence margin defined in operationally meaningful roster-score units; do not present TOST as established practice because no precedent exists in this literature.
- Run 20-30 independent repetitions of any stochastic tuning/selection protocol before making claims about the protocol itself (ParamILS used 25, irace 30, SMAC studies 16), and include two cheap baselines that the literature says must be beaten for the study to be meaningful: the default/production configuration and uniformly random configurations at equal budget (irace warns small-budget tuning can fail to beat random).
- If any experiments run on hardware different from the production Cloud Run environment, verify the final configuration ranking on the production-equivalent machine (Eggensperger Pitfall 7: rankings inverted across machine types); quality-per-move metrics largely immunize us, but any wall-clock-coupled parameter (idle cutoff in seconds) re-introduces machine dependence.

**Findings:**

- **[READ-FULL]** irace defines the tuning budget as maxExperiments B, where one experiment = one configuration run on one instance (or an overall computation time). It splits B into N_iter = 2 + round(log2(N_param)) iterations (races), each with budget B_j = (B - B_used)/(N_iter - j + 1), and samples N_j = B_j/(mu + T_each*min(5,j)) candidate configurations per race. Case-study budgets: 5,000 runs of ACOTSP at 20 CPU-s each, 5,000 runs of MOACO, 10,000 runs of SPEAR at 300 CPU-s cutoff.
  - _Source:_ López-Ibáñez, Dubois-Lacoste, Pérez Cáceres, Birattari, Stützle (2016). The irace package: Iterated racing for automatic algorithm configuration. Operations Research Perspectives 3:43-58. doi:10.1016/j.orp.2016.09.002 (read via IRIDIA mirror PDF)
  - _Relevance:_ Gives us the field's standard unit for reporting tuning effort: number of target-algorithm runs, not wall-clock. ## Statistical methodology for comparing stochastic algorithms

**State of knowledge:** This theme is unusually well settled at the level of published methodology, and thin exactly where our study wants to go. Firmly established: (1) the unit-of-analysis rule - block by instance, test on per-instance paired summaries, never pool seeds (Campelo & Takahashi's pseudoreplication argument; Birattari's variance result that instances dominate seeds for class-level inference); (2) a complete, replicable recipe for choosing both sample sizes from a prespecified minimally relevant effect size (MRES): SE-controlled adaptive runs per instance with allocation proportional to per-algorithm SDs, and power-based instance counts via the paired-t noncentrality (N≈34 for d*=0.5 at 80% power/alpha=.05 in their worked example), with ~16% inflation for Wilcoxon; (3) multiple-comparison norms for k algorithms over N instances: Friedman omnibus then Shaffer-static or Bergmann-Hommel adjusted p-values for all-pairwise, Holm versus a control, Nemenyi deprecated (Garcia & Herrera, reaffirmed by Carrasco et al. 2020, who add joint nonparametric+Bayesian reporting); (4) distribution-level analysis of randomized search: exponential vs Weibull run-time/quality distributions determine whether restarts help, harm, or are neutral, and comparisons flip with the computational budget (Hoos & Stützle); and (5) a mature best-of-K methodology from the NLP-reproducibility line: closed-form order-statistic curves of expected best-of-n from the empirical CDF (Dodge et al.), plus a documented bias critique and unbiased alternative (Tang et al.).

Contested or absent: equivalence testing in algorithm comparison has exactly one widely cited quantitative precedent, Benavoli et al.'s 1% accuracy ROPE, which is asserted by judgment for classification and explicitly flagged (by them and by Carrasco et al.) as not transferable to other domains; nobody has published a principled equivalence margin for scheduling/rostering objective scores, so our margin will have to be argued from operational meaning (Lakens' four justification strategies are the accepted vocabulary for doing that). Sample-size guidance conditioned on FEW, CORRELATED instances (our situation: one ward, months as base instances, perturbations sharing structure) is essentially absent - Campelo & Takahashi assume instances sampled independently from a class, and the CV-correlation literature (Nadeau-Bengio) is the nearest analogue. Nothing we found applies order statistics + equivalence testing + instance blocking jointly to metaheuristic restart curves, which means our planned analysis would be methodologically novel but assemblable entirely from citable parts.

**Design implications:**
- Prespecify a minimally relevant effect size (MRES) in roster-score units before running anything, justified operationally (e.g., the score of one additional undesirable assignment), and use it both for power calculations and as the TOST/ROPE equivalence margin; document the justification in the paper (Lakens strategies; Benavoli 1% precedent is classification-specific and cannot be imported).
- Make the perturbed instance the unit of analysis: compute per-instance paired differences between configurations/algorithms (averaging seeds within instance), run paired tests on those; never pool seeds x instances as independent observations (pseudoreplication).
- Size the instance set with the Campelo-Takahashi machinery (CAISEr): for d*=0.5, alpha=.05, power .8 expect to need roughly 30-40 perturbed instances; if the instance set is fixed by data availability, publish the power curve (power vs detectable d) instead, as their Experiment 2 does.
- Choose seeds per instance adaptively by a standard-error threshold se* with (se*)^2 <= 0.1 x across-instance variance, allocating runs proportional to each algorithm's per-instance SD (start n0=10-15, cap n_max); this is more defensible and cheaper than uniform 30 seeds.
- Because our perturbed instances derive from a handful of production months, treat base month as a blocking/random factor (or stratify all analyses by month); instances sharing a base month are positively correlated and naive tests will be anticonservative (Nadeau-Bengio/Dietterich lesson).
- For the multi-algorithm comparison (LAHC vs SA vs tabu vs SCHC vs random restart), use Friedman + Shaffer-static (or Bergmann-Hommel, feasible at k=5) adjusted p-values for all-pairwise conclusions, Holm if LAHC is the designated control; do not use Nemenyi; report APVs, effect sizes, and CIs, not bare significance stars.
- For the LAHC parameter sweep (history length L, idle cutoff, move mix), decide upfront whether the goal is selection (then racing such as irace/F-race with its published defaults: Friedman elimination, confidence 0.95, first test after 5 instances is a citable protocol) or estimation of the response surface (then a fixed factorial design with the blocking/correction machinery above); do not mix the two inferentially.
- Analyze best-of-K restarts as a curve, not a point: compute expected-best-of-K vs K from the pooled runs via order statistics (Dodge et al. closed form), use or cite the unbiased expected-maximum estimator (Tang et al., meanmax) rather than the biased plug-in, and explicitly look for budget-dependent winner crossovers; pair this with per-instance solution-quality distribution shape checks (exponential-like tail means restarts neutral, heavy tail means restarts help - Hoos & Stützle), never averaging curves across heterogeneous instances.
- Report equivalence claims in the NHST-x-equivalence 2x2 frame (different/equivalent/both/undetermined) with TOST 90% CIs, optionally mirrored by a Bayesian posterior with the prespecified ROPE and a 0.95 posterior decision threshold, so 'fairly tuned algorithms are practically interchangeable' is a positive tested claim rather than an absence of significance.
- Use CP-SAT as the optimality yardstick rather than extreme-value (Weibull location) estimation of the optimum; the EVT literature itself warns those estimates depend on the sampling algorithm's quality; at most mention EVT as corroboration.
- Verify test assumptions the way the sources do: normality of the sampling distribution of per-instance mean differences via QQ-plots/bootstrap (not raw-data normality), Wilcoxon symmetric-distribution assumption if used, and budget ~16% more instances if planning Wilcoxon (ARE 0.86) as the primary test.
- When writing up, anchor the methodology to Campelo & Takahashi (sample sizes), Garcia & Herrera / Carrasco et al. (multiple comparisons norms), Lakens + Benavoli (equivalence margins), Hoos & Stützle + Dodge/Tang (restart and best-of-K analysis) - every piece of the planned analysis then has a citable precedent, and the novelty claim is their combination on production rostering instances.

**Findings:**

- **[READ-FULL]** Two sample sizes must be distinguished: number of repeated runs per instance (controls ACCURACY of the per-instance performance-difference estimate) and number of instances N (controls POWER of the class-level comparison). The convention of 30 or 50 runs (occasionally 100+) is explicitly criticized as arbitrary: 'There is no established rule on how to estimate either value in the literature on experimental comparison of algorithms.'
  - _Source:_ Campelo & Takahashi, J. Heuristics 25(2):305-338, 2019 (read as arXiv:1808.02997v2)
  - _Relevance:_ Our LAHC tuning study should justify both counts explicitly rather than defaulting to 30 seeds; reviewers of a methods-heavy paper will expect this since this paper is the standard reference.
- **[READ-FULL]** Runs per instance: iteratively add runs until the standard error of the estimated paired difference falls below a preset threshold se*, with the optimal allocation of runs between two algorithms r_opt = n1j/n2j = s1j/s2j (proportional to per-algorithm within-instance SDs; KKT-derived). For percent differences, SE uses a simplified Fieller estimator and r_opt = (s1j/s2j)*sqrt(1+(phi_hat)^2). Recommended initial sample n0: 3-4 for bell-shaped run distributions, ~10 for other symmetric, 20-30 for strongly skewed; also set a per-instance budget cap n_max.
  - _Source:_ Campelo & Takahashi 2019, Sec. 4.1 and 4.6, Algorithm 1
  - _Relevance:_ Directly usable recipe for deciding seeds per (instance, configuration) cell in the LAHC sweep; unequal run counts across algorithms are legitimate and more efficient than forcing equal seeds.
- **[READ-FULL]** Number of instances: with per-instance paired differences phi_j as the observations, the paired t-test is the uniformly most powerful unbiased test; required N* = min N such that t_(1-alpha/2)^(N-1) <= t_(beta*;|ncp*|)^(N-1) with ncp* = d*sqrt(N), where d = delta/sigma_Phi and sigma_Phi^2 = sigma_phi^2 (across-instance) + sigma_eps^2 (within-instance measurement error). Guideline: choose se* so (se*)^2 <= 0.1*sigma_Phi^2 so measurement error does not erode power. Nonparametric fallback via asymptotic relative efficiency: N_wilcoxon = N*/0.86 ≈ 1.16N*, N_sign = N*/0.637 ≈ 1.57N* (Wilcoxon ARE vs t-test ≈0.95 normal, >=0.86 non-normal).
  - _Source:_ Campelo & Takahashi 2019, Secs. 4.2-4.4.2, Eqs. 22-26
  - _Relevance:_ Gives the exact machinery for sizing our perturbed-instance set, and a concrete safety-margin rule if we plan Wilcoxon instead of t-tests on roster scores.
- **[READ-FULL]** Worked example numbers: with alpha=0.05, power target 0.8 (beta*=0.2), MRES d*=0.5, se*=0.05 on percent differences, n0=15, n_max=200, bootstrap SE (R=999), the method required N*=34 instances; per-instance run counts self-adjusted between 15 and ~120 per algorithm (3 of 34 instances hit the n_max=200 total budget). Result: mean IGD difference -0.379, p=2.90e-6, CI95 [-0.517,-0.242]. Second experiment with predefined N=200 instances instead published a power curve: power 0.5 at d=0.12, 0.8 at d=0.18, 0.95 at d=0.23 (one-sided paired t).
  - _Source:_ Campelo & Takahashi 2019, Sec. 5, Tables 2, Figs. 1 and 4
  - _Relevance:_ Benchmark magnitudes: ~34 instances for d*=0.5 at 80% power is the scale our perturbed-instance design should target; with a fixed instance set we should publish the power curve for the achievable effect sizes, exactly as their Experiment 2 does.
- **[READ-FULL]** Using individual runs (seeds) as if they were independent observations in class-level tests is pseudoreplication: 'the independent observations ... are the individual values phi_j ... and not the individual runs of the algorithms on each instance. Failure to realize this point leads to pseudoreplication, i.e., the calculation of test statistics under falsely inflated degrees-of-freedom, with a consequent loss of control over the statistical error rates.'
  - _Source:_ Campelo & Takahashi 2019, Sec. 4.2 (citing Hurlbert 1984, Lazic 2010)
  - _Relevance:_ Hard constraint on our analysis: block by instance, average (or model) seeds within instance; never pool 10 seeds x 20 instances as n=200.
- **[READ-FULL]** TOST equivalence testing: declare equivalence if two one-sided tests against bounds (-DeltaL, DeltaU) both reject at alpha, equivalently if the 90% CI (=1-2alpha) of the difference lies inside the bounds. Sample sizes per group for symmetric bounds at alpha=.05: d=0.3 needs 191 (80% power) / 241 (90%); d=0.4: 108/136; d=0.5: 70/88; d=0.8: 28/35. Dependent (paired) designs need half as many pairs. Bound-setting strategies: (a) resource-based smallest detectable effect (e.g., n=100/group, 80% power gives SESOI d=0.414), (b) small-telescopes (effect the original study had 33% power to detect), (c) benchmark d=.2/.5/.8 as last resort, (d) theory-specified.
  - _Source:_ Lakens, Social Psychological and Personality Science 8(4):355-362, 2017 (PMC5502906, OA)
  - _Relevance:_ If we claim 'LAHC is practically equivalent to SA under fair tuning', TOST with a prespecified margin is the frequentist instrument; the paired-design halving matters because our comparisons are paired by instance. Margin must be prespecified and justified, not post hoc.
- **[READ-FULL]** Four possible outcomes when combining NHST and equivalence tests (different from zero and/or within bounds), including 'statistically different AND statistically equivalent' (trivially small but reliable difference); 108 SPPS articles had concluded 'no effect' from nonsignificance alone, which equivalence testing corrects.
  - _Source:_ Lakens 2017
  - _Relevance:_ Structure our conclusions in this 2x2 frame; especially likely in our setting where large seed counts can make trivially small score differences significant.
- **[READ-FULL]** For all-pairwise comparison of k algorithms over N problems: Friedman ranks with z=(Ri-Rj)/sqrt(k(k+1)/6N), then Nemenyi (alpha/m, m=k(k-1)/2) is 'very conservative ... we do not recommend'; Holm (alpha/(m-i+1)) acceptable when many datasets; Shaffer static (alpha/t_i using logically-possible-truth counts) 'strongly encouraged' at little extra complexity; Bergmann-Hommel (exhaustive sets) is the most powerful but computationally intensive (exhaustive-set counts: k=5 gives 51 sets, k=9 gives 21,146). Case study (5 classifiers, 30 datasets, Friedman chi2=39.647, Iman-Davenport F=14.309): of 10 pairwise hypotheses, Nemenyi rejects 4, Holm 5, Shaffer 6, Bergmann-Hommel 8. Report adjusted p-values (APVs) rather than bare alphas.
  - _Source:_ Garcia & Herrera, JMLR 9:2677-2694, 2008 (OA)
  - _Relevance:_ Sets the multiple-comparison norm for our 5-algorithm cross-comparison (k=5 is exactly their case-study size): use Shaffer static or Bergmann-Hommel APVs for all-pairwise, Holm if comparing against LAHC as control. For parameter sweeps treat each configuration as an 'algorithm' and the same machinery applies.
- **[READ-PARTIAL]** Bayesian alternative with explicit equivalence margins: ROPE (region of practical equivalence) of [-0.01, 0.01] on mean accuracy difference, justified only as 'in classification, it is sensible to define that two classifiers whose mean difference of accuracies is less than 1% are practically equivalent', with footnote 'in other domains a different value could be more suitable'; decisions made when a posterior probability (left/rope/right) exceeds 0.95. Also: correlated t-test for cross-validation uses Nadeau-Bengio heuristic rho=n_te/n_tot because rho is not identifiable (MLE is 0 regardless of data). Case study on 54 UCI datasets: NHST significant in 19/54; Bayesian-with-ROPE finds 12 practically better, 6 practically equivalent, rest undecided.
  - _Source:_ Benavoli, Corani, Demsar, Zaffalon, JMLR 18(77):1-36, 2017 (OA; pp.1-14 read)
  - _Relevance:_ The main published precedent for an equivalence margin in algorithm comparison; note the margin was asserted by domain judgment, not derived - so our 9-component roster score needs its own operationally justified margin (e.g., score value of one violated soft constraint). The 0.95 posterior decision rule is a usable template.
- **[READ-PARTIAL]** Modern metaheuristics-specific guidance (CEC'17 case study): use parametric tests 'only if the normality and homoscedasticity prerequisites are fulfilled'; encourages 'the joint use of non-parametric and Bayesian tests' since nonparametric tests detect differences but Bayesian posteriors quantify them; warns the ROPE=0.01 default 'was set ... in the original proposal for a classification scenario' and may under-represent data in other domains; prior choice remains a subjective decision. Example output: Bayesian signed-rank on CEC'17 dim-100 shows DES beating all others with posterior probabilities 0.59 (vs LSCNE) to 0.97 (vs PPSO).
  - _Source:_ Carrasco, Garcia, Rueda, Das, Herrera, Swarm and Evolutionary Computation 54:100665, 2020 (read as arXiv:2002.09227, pp.40-52 + abstract)
  - _Relevance:_ Confirms the field's current norm is nonparametric + optional Bayesian dual reporting; supplies the argument for defining our own ROPE rather than importing 1%.
- **[METADATA-ONLY]** Budget allocation across instances vs runs: Birattari showed the variance-optimal use of a fixed budget of total runs for estimating expected performance on an instance CLASS is one run per instance on as many instances as possible ('running each algorithm a single time on each instance if needed'); Campelo & Takahashi note this precludes per-instance analysis and ignores power/MRES considerations. Companion note (Birattari & Dorigo 2007, Optim. Lett. 1:309-311) formally refutes reporting best-of-N runs, since best-of-N estimates an N-dependent quantity rather than algorithm quality.
  - _Source:_ Birattari, IRIDIA TR/2004-001, 2004; Birattari & Dorigo, Optimization Letters 1(3):309-311, 2007 (both paywalled/403; content via Campelo & Takahashi 2019's summary and abstracts)
  - _Relevance:_ Two-sided lesson: (a) our replication effort should favor more perturbed instances over more seeds when estimating class-level means; (b) if we report best-of-K restarts we must treat K as an explicit design variable, never as a headline number - which is exactly what the best-of-K curve methodology does.
- **[READ-PARTIAL]** Racing as sequential statistics for tuning (published defaults): irace eliminates candidate configurations using the Friedman-test-based F-test by default (t-test variants available: t-test, t-test-bonferroni, t-test-holm; t-test auto-selected when capping is on), at confidence 0.95, with the first elimination test only after firstTest=5 instances and tests after every eachTest=1 instance thereafter; elitist racing on by default (elitistNewInstances=1).
  - _Source:_ Lopez-Ibanez et al., 'The irace package', Operations Research Perspectives 3:43-58, 2016; defaults verified from CRAN irace reference manual
  - _Relevance:_ If our tuning budget forces adaptive designs, F-race/irace is the citable protocol (blocking by instance via Friedman is built in); alternatively we can defend a fixed full-factorial design precisely because we want estimation (response surfaces over L, idle cutoff, move mix), which racing does not provide.
- **[READ-FULL]** Run-time/solution-quality distributions, not means: an RTD example with mean=SD=100s gives cutoff estimates for 99% success of 10,000s (Markov, mean only), 1,100s (Chebyshev, mean+SD) vs 460s (known exponential RTD) - 'analyzing only means and standard deviations is a very wasteful use of empirical data.' Empirical RTDs of well-parameterized SLS algorithms on hard instances pass chi-squared tests for exponentiality; exponential RTD implies P(success in next k steps) is independent of elapsed steps, so restarts at arbitrary cutoffs give no benefit and p independent parallel runs give optimal speedup. Below-optimal noise parameters yield Weibull RTDs with shape<1 (heavier tail: restarts help); steeper-than-exponential RTDs mean restarts hurt. RTDs of two algorithms crossed at ~420 steps (optimal cutoff for one ~170 steps; restart at optimal cutoff gave ~24% speedup); averaging RTDs over an inhomogeneous instance set manufactures a 'virtual optimal cutoff' (~750 steps) that exists for no single instance.
  - _Source:_ Hoos & Stuetzle, 'Evaluating Las Vegas Algorithms - Pitfalls and Remedies', UAI 1998, pp.238-245 (arXiv:1301.7383)
  - _Relevance:_ Foundational for our best-of-K restart analysis: (a) fit/inspect per-instance quality- and time-distributions before recommending restart policies; (b) whether best-of-K restarts beat one long LAHC run depends on the tail shape of the single-run distribution; (c) never average restart curves across heterogeneous instances without blocking - conclusions about optimal K can be artifacts.
- **[READ-FULL]** Best-of-K curves via order statistics: expected best of n i.i.d. draws estimated in closed form from the empirical CDF, E_hat[V*_n] = sum_v v*(P_hat(V<=v)^n - P_hat(V<v)^n), which 'has strictly less error than using the bootstrap' (no resampling error); at n=1 it equals the mean, at n=N the observed max, so the curve lower-bounds asymptotic best performance. Case studies show budget-dependent winners: logistic regression beats CNN on SST for budgets under ~10 random-search trials, CNN wins for larger budgets (50 trials run); GloVe-only is best under 2h compute, frozen ELMo best from ~6h-1day, fine-tuned ELMo best at >=10 days. Of 50 randomly chosen EMNLP 2018 papers, none reported all recommended items (budget, search space, validation scores, expected-best curve).
  - _Source:_ Dodge, Gururangan, Card, Schwartz, Smith, EMNLP 2019, pp.2185-2194 (ACL Anthology D19-1224, OA)
  - _Relevance:_ This is the established template for analyzing our best-of-K restart data: compute expected-best-of-K curves per algorithm from the same pool of runs (no extra compute), and report winner-vs-budget crossovers rather than a single K. Directly transfers from hyperparameter trials to LAHC restarts.
- **[READ-PARTIAL]** Caveat on the plug-in best-of-n estimator: it 'is biased and uses error-prone assumptions', 'favors negative errors and yields poor bootstrapped confidence intervals'; the authors derive an unbiased alternative for the expected maximum (code: castorini/meanmax).
  - _Source:_ Tang, Lee, Xin, Liu, Yu, Lin, 'Showing Your Work Doesn't Always Work', ACL 2020 (aclanthology 2020.acl-main.246)
  - _Relevance:_ If we publish best-of-K curves, use or at least cite the unbiased estimator (the standard unbiased expected-maximum estimator weights order statistics by C(i-1,n-1)/C(N,n)); reviewers aware of this exchange may probe the estimator choice.
- **[METADATA-ONLY]** Statistical estimation of the unknown optimum from heuristic samples: a review concludes the field uses Weibull/extreme-value (Fisher-Tippett: minima of samples approach a 3-parameter Weibull whose location parameter estimates the optimum) and jackknife estimators; a 2022 empirical study asks whether the global optimum 'can be reliably estimated through extreme value theory' and finds estimates and their confidence intervals depend on the performance of the search algorithm that generated the sample, i.e., reliable bounds require high-quality solution samples.
  - _Source:_ Giddings, Rardin, Uzsoy, J. Heuristics 20:329-358, 2014 (paywalled, abstract only); 'Can the global optimum of a combinatorial optimization problem be reliably estimated through extreme value theory?', Swarm and Evolutionary Computation, 2022 (paywalled, abstract only)
  - _Relevance:_ Tempting complement to CP-SAT bounds (Weibull-location estimate of the optimum from restart samples), but the literature itself warns the estimate is algorithm-dependent; since we have CP-SAT as the optimality yardstick we should prefer it and use EVT only as a secondary curiosity, if at all.
- **[READ-PARTIAL]** Racing/estimation heritage aside, the standard cross-validation-style correlation correction (Nadeau-Bengio rho) exists because overlapping training data breaks run independence; in our optimization setting the analogous danger is perturbed instances derived from the same production month being treated as independent when they share structure - Benavoli et al. show unadjusted t-tests are 'not calibrated' with Type I error 'much larger than alpha' under such correlation (citing Dietterich 1998).
  - _Source:_ Benavoli et al. 2017, Sec. 2 (Nadeau & Bengio, Machine Learning 52:239-281, 2003)
  - _Relevance:_ Perturbations of the same base roster month are pseudo-instances with induced correlation; either model month as a random effect / stratify perturbations by base month, or expect inflated false positives.

## Nurse and physician rostering: benchmarks, comparisons, deployed systems

**State of knowledge:** The rostering field has a mature benchmark culture (INRC-I 2010, INRC-II 2015, schedulingbenchmarks.org) with standardized instance descriptors (staff count x horizon weeks, skills, contracts, shift types, weighted integer soft-constraint scores) and a settled empirical hierarchy on those benchmarks: mathematical programming (branch-and-price, multi-commodity-flow IP, two-phase IP decompositions) won both nurse rostering competitions outright, with well-tuned SA/large-neighborhood metaheuristics trailing by roughly 4-15% in average cost but scaling better and occasionally finding best-known solutions; ejection-chain/VDS heuristics match proven optima on easy real instances and degrade several-fold on hard ones under equal budgets. What the field firmly knows about methodology is largely negative: the two major reviews (Van den Bergh 2013, ~300 papers; Erhard 2018, 68 physician papers) and the 2025 IEEE Access physician-scheduling review (60 papers) all document that real-world studies use single-institution, non-public data; that algorithm comparisons are typically against self-implemented baselines with one hand-chosen configuration and seed; and that standardized comparative validation is an explicitly unmet research call. Statistically principled tuning exists but only at the margins: F-Race-tuned SA on INRC-II competition instances (Ceschia & Schaerf, 99% confidence) and scattered irace use; we found no study combining rigorous tuning with a fair multi-algorithm comparison on production instances from a deployed system, and no rostering study tuning LAHC specifically.

Deployed-system papers form a separate genre with a different evidence standard: ROTA (2021) reports scheduling time falling 45→10 min/day and 91% satisfaction (191/210 nurses); HealthRota (2022) reports SUS median 86/100 from 209 respondents with Kruskal-Wallis tests; the 2025 physician-rostering MIP framework reports 12 monthly instances solved to a 3% CBC gap in <9 min with quality 'largely identical' to incumbent department-specific models and months of production use. None of these performs algorithmic comparison or tuning; conversely, the algorithmically rigorous papers are not deployed. On fairness, weighted-sum objectives (our 9-component pattern) are confirmed as the dominant formulation (Fuchs et al. 2025, 97 papers), but the fairness literature (Wolbeck 2019; Ouelhadj et al. 2012) warns that total-penalty weighted sums produce solver-dependent fairness, and recommends reporting independent post-hoc fairness measures (min-max spread, std dev, Jain index), which most papers omit. Contested/absent: whether metaheuristic rankings transfer from competition to production instances (untested), per-instance-family parameter sensitivity (Ceschia & Schaerf note their best configuration differed by horizon and suggest per-horizon tuning, but did not pursue it), and any statistically characterized tuning surface for LAHC-family parameters in rostering.

**Design implications:**
- Our ~10-15 real+perturbed instance count is normal-to-generous for practice-based rostering studies (12 monthly instances in the most recent deployed physician-rostering paper; single-hospital data in most of 60 PSP studies); frame it as such, and document the perturbation model explicitly (precedent: Moz & Pato's 68 absence-perturbed instances).
- Adopt the competition adjudication protocol as our comparison backbone: one fixed published scoring function (like INRC-II's integer weights), equal calibrated time budgets per algorithm, >=10 independent seeds per instance-algorithm pair, per-instance rank aggregation plus raw avg/best cost tables — this is the only comparison protocol the field treats as fair.
- Include CP-SAT bounds for every instance and report metaheuristic gap-to-bound: MP methods won both INRCs, so any 'LAHC/SA is good enough' claim is only credible against proven bounds (Burke & Curtois report LB at root node per instance; Knust & Xie compute MIP bounds for exactly this purpose).
- Report results at multiple time budgets (e.g., 30 s and 10 min, the practitioner budgets elicited by Burke & Curtois, plus our production budget): INRC-I ran three time-budget tracks precisely because algorithm ranking is budget-dependent; our idle-cutoff and best-of-K questions should be answered per budget.
- Use a racing method (F-Race/irace) or an equally defensible statistical design for the LAHC tuning, and cite Ceschia & Schaerf's F-Race-at-99%-confidence as the precedent; explicitly contrast with the field default of one hand-chosen configuration and single seed (Burke & Curtois say so verbatim) — that contrast is a publishable contribution in itself.
- Characterize instances using the field's descriptor convention: number of doctors, horizon days, shift/duty types, skills/qualifications, contract rules, request counts, coverage requirements, and hard/soft constraint list with weights; consider releasing anonymized instances, since dataset non-availability is the most-cited gap in both physician-scheduling reviews.
- Keep the 9-component weighted score fixed across all algorithms (mirrors the INRC single-validator design), but additionally report independent post-hoc fairness statistics per roster (max/range/std dev of per-doctor burden, optionally Jain index): the fairness literature explicitly criticizes weighted-sum-only objectives as solver-dependent fairness, and reviewers from HCMS/ORHC may raise this.
- Present deployment evidence in the genre's accepted currency alongside the algorithmic study: time-to-roster before/after, operator satisfaction, months in production use, roster quality vs previous manual/incumbent process (ROTA, HealthRota, and the arXiv MIP framework establish this evidence standard); the combination of both evidence types in one paper is rare and differentiating.
- Before claiming novelty ('first statistically rigorous tuning/comparison on production rostering instances'), obtain and check Knust & Xie (2019) full text and Kletzander & Musliu (2020), the two closest paywalled analogues; phrase the claim as 'to our knowledge' scoped to physician rostering and deployed systems.
- Expect and preempt the finding that best parameters differ by instance family: Ceschia & Schaerf found optimal kmax and configuration differed between 4-week and 8-week horizons and suggested per-horizon configurations; testing parameter stability across our real vs perturbed instances directly addresses an open question in the literature.
- Do not oversell metaheuristic wins on weighted scores with large weight steps: Burke & Curtois show objective values become misleading when weights differ by 1000x (WHPP anomaly); since our 9 components likely have disparate weights, also report per-component violation counts, not just total score.

- **[READ-FULL]** Working-paper survey of fairness in personnel scheduling formalizes 8 objective types: [1] min sum of per-employee penalties, [2] min-max (most widespread genuine fairness measure), [3] min range, [4] min mean, [5] min sum of |deviation from mean|, [6] min std dev, [7] min sum of squared penalties, [8] max % fulfilled requests. Key critique: plain weighted-sum minimization of total penalties ([1]) gives fairness that is 'solver-dependent' (Gross et al. 2019) and 'does not adequately reflect a fairness objective' (Ouelhadj et al. 2012), because nothing prevents violations concentrating on the same individuals.
  - _Source:_ Wolbeck, 'Fairness Aspects in Personnel Scheduling', FU Berlin Discussion Paper 2019/18 (free PDF via d-nb.info)
  - _Relevance:_ Our 9-component weighted score is type [1]; reviewers versed in this literature may probe fairness — we should report per-doctor distribution statistics (max, range, std dev) post-hoc even while optimizing the fixed weighted score.
- **[READ-FULL]** Review of 97 papers (2013-2024) on fairness in hospital personnel planning: the weighted sum is 'the most prevalent approach' for combining fairness with efficiency objectives; measurement taxonomy = one/spread (min max deviation from target), all/spread (sum of absolute/pairwise deviations — most common), all/position (weighted powers of metrics); 'most literature does not introduce an independent fairness measure', which the authors flag as a cheap, unexplored opportunity (e.g., Jain index, variance) for robust cross-paper comparison; envy-based fairness concepts are entirely absent from healthcare scheduling.
  - _Source:_ Fuchs, Schimmelpfeng, Brunner, 'A roadmap for integrating fairness in personnel planning and scheduling in hospitals', Operations Research, Data Analytics and Logistics 45:200479, 2025 (open access; Augsburg OPUS PDF)
  - _Relevance:_ Confirms our weighted-sum design is mainstream, and gives a concrete, near-zero-cost differentiator: report an independent fairness measure (Jain index / std dev of per-doctor burden) alongside the 9-component score.
- **[READ-FULL]** Rostering competitions' adjudication is the field's only standardized fair-comparison protocol: per-instance ranking of objective values across solvers, averaged over instances, with finals using organiser-run 10 random-seed trials per instance on hidden instances and calibrated per-machine time budgets; imported from ITC-2007 via INRC-I to INRC-II. Outside competitions, comparative practice is one-config/one-seed (Burke & Curtois 2014) or comparison against self-implemented 'basic tabu search or simulated annealing' (Van den Bergh et al. 2013). No paper found performs statistically rigorous parameter tuning AND fair multi-algorithm comparison on production (deployed-system) instances; the closest are F-Race-tuned SA on INRC-II competition instances (Ceschia & Schaerf 2018) and irace-configured evolutionary methods on mixed random/real instance sets (Swarm & Evolutionary Computation 2025, abstract only).
  - _Source:_ Synthesis across INRC-I/II rules (arXiv:1501.04177), Burke & Curtois 2014, Van den Bergh et al. 2013, Ceschia & Schaerf PATAT 2018
  - _Relevance:_ This is the precise gap our study fills; the negative claim ('no such study found') is defensible if phrased as 'to our knowledge' after also checking Knust & Xie's full text.
- **[READ-FULL]** Typical instance counts in published rostering studies: benchmark studies use ~25-40 instances (Burke & Curtois: ~26 + 60 competition instances; Ceschia & Schaerf: ~40 INRC-II; INRC-II testbed: 14 datasets); real-world/deployment studies use far fewer — 12 monthly instances (arXiv 2511.14536), single-department case studies over several planning periods (Fügener et al. anesthesia case study), one hospital's data (most of the 60 PSP studies). Perturbation precedent exists: Moz & Pato generated 68 random test instances mimicking worker absences from a real base instance (cited approvingly by Van den Bergh et al.).
  - _Source:_ Synthesis: Burke & Curtois 2014; Ceschia & Schaerf 2018; arXiv:2511.14536; Van den Bergh et al. 2013; Abdullah et al. 2025
  - _Relevance:_ Our planned ~10-15 real + perturbed production instances is normal-to-generous for practice-based studies; the Moz & Pato precedent legitimizes perturbed-instance generation if the perturbation model is documented.
